Case Study 001

Park Avenue Bakery

Helena, Montana — Artisan bakery, single location

The Challenge

Park Avenue Bakery is a beloved Helena institution baking specialty breads, cookies, and bars six days a week. Their operation ran on phone calls, walk-ins, and institutional knowledge — which worked until demand outgrew the system. No online ordering. No structured prep process. No way to manage capacity during peak periods. Staff relied on memory for daily checklists, and there was no visibility into whether opening and closing procedures were being completed consistently.

What We Built

E-Commerce Ordering System

Full shopping cart with category filtering, localStorage persistence, real-time totals, and Clover Hosted Checkout payment integration via Vercel serverless functions.

Intelligent Order Guardrails

Day-specific menu availability tied to their actual baking schedule — 7 specialty breads rotating across the week. Automated cutoff times (5 PM day-before for specialty, 10 AM same-day for everyday items). Daily inventory caps per item type. Blackout dates for holidays. Per-order quantity limits with large-order phone redirects.

Staff Prep Dashboard

Password-protected dashboard showing today's bake list generated from incoming orders, pickup schedule organized by time slot, "Mark Ready" tracking, and printable pickup sheets for counter staff. Auto-refreshes throughout the day.

Operational Checklists

Five daily checklists covering every shift from 4 AM baker opening to 5:30 PM closing — 59 items total. Multiple input types including checkboxes, temperature readings with out-of-range alerts, photo proof uploads, and numeric entries. Progress tracking and save/resume for interrupted workflows. Touch-optimized for kitchen use.

Tech Stack

HTML5, CSS3, Vanilla JavaScript (ES6+), Vercel Serverless Functions, Clover Hosted Checkout API, Formspree, localStorage/sessionStorage. No frameworks, no bloat — just fast, purpose-built tools that the team can understand and maintain.

The Takeaway

The best operations systems aren't impressive — they're invisible. They let the team focus on baking, not firefighting.
